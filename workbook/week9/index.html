general feelings

Coming into week 9, I'm feeling excited to finally get going on my final project. I think that for me, the struggle is going to be (as it always is) refining and reducing my scope to be managable and achievable within the time frame. With there being just so so many possible directions for this brief, this is definitely going to be a bit of a challenge, but I think if I can set the intention to have a smaller scope early on, then I should hopefully be able to follow through with it. I think I will also have to focus on getting my head around the physical computing stuff as early on as I can, because if I want to use it (and I do) then this is going to continue to be a big learning curve and challenge for me.

activities

presenting in class

I presented my pitch for my final work in class using this script:

For my final project, I have decided to build a tent. The tent. My highest priority for this project was the idea of affect and so I began by considering what kind of feeling I wanted to tap into, or what would be the most potent trigger for this feeling. My thoughts ran wild - I considered dancing, yelling, singing, the quiet, warm feeling that you get just before you fall asleep. I wanted to make something that would either spark these feelings or inspire a user to actually get up and do them. But at this moment in my life, on the brink of finishing my degree, and with absolutely no idea what I will do next, nothing feels quite as real or as close as my fear of the unknown.

So, I've decided that I need a break. I need to let myself feel like a kid again - because that's all I really am! I need to let myself, once again find delight and wonder in being small and out of control. Call it escapism, but I think we all need a little more magic and mystery, and so I set about looking for a way to find this feeling.

When I was a kid, I spent almost all of my time making potions and casting spells in my fairy garden. I was obsessed with my jewellery boxes filled with trinkets and precious, secret, tiny things. I had locks on all my diaries and gaze at the special rocks and flowers that I collected. I think this feeling of quiet, secret, special contemplation and collection is really integral to a lot of people's childhoods. When I remember the quiet whispers that I spoke to myself and all the tiny invisible fairies around me, I almost still believe that they're real.

So, with these goals in mind, I began to ideate inputs and outputs. What I have come up with is an installation - a tent filled with warm glowing light and the sounds of whispers and laughter. Sensors and speakers at each corner of the tent will mean that no matter where you stand, the words being spoken will always be just out of earshot. Kids can go into the tent straight away and hear the mysterious words, but for an adult to participate, they must leave behind all electronic devices, crawl into a sleeping bag, and truly believe, and trust, that magic is still real.

I am, of course, drawing from the quilting works of Tracey Emin, who has her own tent testament to loss of innocence and growing up in her work Everyone I Have Ever Slept With. I have also drawn inspiration from Levi Felix, who was a tech founder that had a breakdown while working 70-hour work weeks, and then created Camp Grounded, a summer camp for adults. One of the most delightful elements of the unplugging process at this camp is that he would subject his visitors to ridiculous rituals and tests any time they wanted to use their phone.

Becky took notes on feedback that was given to me by the class and Karen ann, which you can see in the photos below. Basically, the feedback suggested that I have a strong central idea and storytelling, but that I will need to focus on clarifying my inputs and outputs, and that the design aspect can come later. There were also suggestions that I should be purposeful when picking the media that will be part of my inputs and outputs ie. what is the content of the 'whispering'?

paper prototyping

The paper prototyping activity involved producing a little paper model of my installation, and using this to consider size (in relation to a person), and attempt to refine my inputs and outputs. I made my little tent out of a patchwork of textile images that I found in magazines, and then used pom poms to represent pillows and blankets that made me surrounding or filling the installation. I also made a little human out of paper to show scale.

I did find this exercise helpful in beginning to refine what I wanted to actual, physical experience of my piece to be, but I am honestly still pretty unsure. I think that not knowing how to practically achieve any of the physical computing elements is really throwing me off and making me feel quite overwhelmed by this whole prospect. I will need to continue to think about and experiement with my ideas in order to figure this all out, and feel like I have a handle on things...

I considered making the tent itself interactive, through the use of conductive thread that reacts when touched, by dimming or changing the lights of audio elements. I also considered using a camera as an input and projecting on the outside or inside of the tent based on this inout. Karen ann suggests using an RFID to enhance the experience of exploring a collection of objects inside the tent - perhaps something lights up when you touch it, or when it is placed on a particular place.

I'm wondering, at this point, if focusing on a 'collection' of special items - perhaps housed in a jewellery box or something - would be a more focussed way to approach my concept.

case studies

your ai slop bores me

This is a funny little site set entirely in comic sans, where you can make requests from an 'AI', but it's actually just a human on the other side. You spend credits when you ask questions (or ask for drawings) and you can earn them back only by 'larping' as an AI yourself, and answering other people's questions or requests for drawings. I played from both sides, and it was pretty beautiful I have to say! I enjoyed being the AI the most, because I found it interesting seeing what people were asking. Some people were clearly just there for a giggle, but some people had actually sent through real questions, like 'how do I ask for a promotion?', and these ones made me feel so happy - that people were asking each other, even in this silly anonymous way, rather than asking a machine.

I think this site works in both situations; when I was feeling silly, I asked for a drawing of a girl with a muffin and received on that is so delightful omg! and then I asked what to do when you feel overwhelmed by responsibilities, and got a message back about breaking down tasks one by one. Even though this answer was relatively banal and unhelpful, the fact that it was coming from another person felt special and real. I really really like this site and I think everyone should try larping as an AI chat bot. It's one of those artworks that makes you think, 'Damn, I wish I had done that'.

Tracey Emin

Tracey Emin is an artist whose work in textiles - particularly patchwork and quilting- has long been an inspiration to me. For the ideation of my final piece, I am, of course, drawing on her quilting works, and her own tent testament to loss of innocence and growing up: Everyone I Have Ever Slept With. This tent is really funny and witty, but also quite poignant and beautiful, I think.

Lit up from the inside, the tent's interior is covered by a patchwork of the names of Emin's lovers. The space of the tent evokes the intimacy, heat and bodily nature of the act itself, while feeling juvenile in a way, like she has snuck out and had sex in a tent because she wasn't allowed to have people over. The patchwork as a medium is also really interesting; it suggests something about the passage of time, as if each name added has been placed to mend or embelish the fabric at different moments in time. Her work is not delicate or dainty, it is loud and decisive in its visual language, and does not shrink away from possible criticism.

I also love her installation, My Bed, which is just the artist's bed and surroundings transplanted out of her home and into the gallery space. The piece includes dirty sheets, old soft toys, empty liquor bottles and cigarette butts. It's honestly pretty gross. But you can feel the way the owner of that bed would be feeling. The chaos, perhaps self disgust, and depression that could be associated with sleeping in these circumstances.

Levi Felix

I also looked at Levi Felix's Digital Detox/Camp Grounded, a summer camp for adults, when considering binging in elements of ritual and performance to my artwork. When entering this phone free space, people were required to undergo a ridiculous series of tests performed by people in hazmat suits, that ritually functioned as a physical way to detox from digital devices. While there was no tangible reasons for these performances of detox, they served a ritual function, forcing people to stop for a moment and really consider the decision to pick their phone back up. I like the idea of somehow enforcing a pause before entering the tent. I would like to ask people to stop and consider where they are in that particular moment. This is obviously a very common practice in mindfulness and yoga, but I like Felix's novel, comic twist.
