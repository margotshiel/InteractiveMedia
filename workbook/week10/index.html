Play a Grill

We looked at Aisen Caro Chacin's Play a Grill in class a few weeks ago, but I'm coming back to it now, after having seen it on the extensive Interactive Media milanote board. This piece takes the grill - which Chacin describes as a music fashion object, a decorative plate worn (generally) by hip hop and rap artists - and transforms it into a personal music listening device. It takes the unique opportunity of the grill's placement over the teeth and transmits sound through the teeth (bone conduction hearing). The grill itself can be styled to look however the user desires, as this decorative element has no bearing on the technology, thus allowing the grill to retain its cultural meaning and each artist's individual expression through this higly specific, and personal piece of jewellery. Chacin has even embedded a control panel into the underside of the plate, which can be used to change the music being played, with the wearer's tongue.

I really love this project. Grillz are such an important cultural phenomenon, and Chacin's extension of their capabilities is handled so delicately. The link between music and teeth is so specific, and it comes together so elegantly here. I also quite like the aesthetic of teeth in general - I once made my mum a pair of earrings from my baby teeth that she has collected, and she wears them all the time! They look like little pieces of porcelain, and remind me of Myer Wolfsheim's molar cufflinks in The Great Gatsby. I wonder if there is something here that I could explore? The tooth fairy... my collections of talismans as a child, vs my mum's collection of her children's baby teeth? I think there is something there.
